Do RNA-FM embeddings beat hand-crafted features?

A linear probe on frozen RNA-FM representations, scored against the 16 thermodynamic features from Kundlatsch et al., on identical folds and an identical target.

n 582 terminators target ln(TS) cv 10-fold, seed 42 backbone frozen throughout 2026-09-04

The answer is no — they tie.

Best RNA-FM pipeline scores 0.666, best hand-crafted pipeline 0.620. The difference is +0.046 against a fold-to-fold spread of ±0.13, winning 8 of 10 folds at p = 0.13. Not a difference this dataset can resolve.

That is still worth knowing: 640 generic dimensions from a model that has never seen a terminator match 16 features designed from decades of termination biochemistry. The pretrained representation contains the signal — it just doesn't contain more of it.

The 19 nt downstream of the termination site carries real signal. One-hot gains the most from it (+0.085 grouped, 9/10 folds) — and one-hot is a purely linear model on raw nucleotides, so the information is unambiguously present rather than an artifact of the embedding.

Layer 12 mean-pooled is the only arm that cannot use it (−0.006, 5/10 folds). That motivated the pooling ablation.

Layer 12 holds the signal near the 3′ end — narrowing to the last 30 positions gains +0.050 (10/10 folds, p = 0.002). Averaging over all 109 really was diluting it.

Layer 6 does not behave that way at all. Narrowing gains nothing; it wants a second statistic over the same positions. mean+max wins 10/10 folds at p = 0.002.

Two negative results worth keeping. flatten — 69,760 features, free positional weighting — does not win, so ridge cannot exploit that freedom at n = 582 and pooling is acting as useful regularization. And mean downstream only matches the full window at layer 6: those 19 positions already encode the upstream through self-attention.

Same features, same model, same folds. On raw TS the grouping penalty is 0.24; on ln(TS) it is 0.07.

R² on raw TS is dominated by a handful of terminators in the 200–380 range, so a fold's score depends mostly on where those few landed — hence fold standard deviations up to ±0.45 and one fold at −0.44. On a log target no single terminator dominates the squared error and folds become comparable. Most of what looked like leakage was target skew.

Caveats

What not to over-read

The pooling winner was selected post hoc. Ten poolings × two layers × two schemes = 40 configurations, and mean+max at layer 6 was the best of them. With fold sd near 0.13, some of that +0.032 is selection. Re-run it on a fresh fold seed before it carries a claim.

  • GroupKFold is one deterministic partition. Every ± here is across folds, not across repeats. GroupShuffleSplit over several seeds would give a real error bar.
  • Groups use exact hairpin identity. 101 pairs of distinct hairpins are ≥90% similar, so similarity clustering would merge more rows and push the grouped numbers lower.
  • Hand-crafted features predate the annotation fix. They were computed before the pheA-1 / ECK120034435 swap, so 2 of 582 rows carry uncorrected values in that arm.
  • Assay noise is unmeasured. Chen's sheets carry per-construct standard deviations. Until that noise floor is computed, the ceiling on achievable R² is unknown — some of the remaining variance may not be explainable by any model.
Next

Where the headroom is

Not in better pooling of frozen embeddings. Across 40 configurations the ceiling sat at 0.62–0.67 regardless of layer, window or pooling, and the best linear probe ties a tuned gradient-boosted model on 16 hand-designed features.

That is the expected result for a frozen backbone, and it was the right cheap first experiment. If a foundation model is going to earn its keep here it will be through fine-tuning or the domain-adaptive continued pre-training on terminator corpora already proposed in the project README — not through a better read-out of representations that were never adapted to this task.
